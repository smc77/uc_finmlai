# Final-Project Report Pathways

The final project is a piece of financial research, not merely a model and not
merely a code submission. Every student must conduct and communicate a credible
empirical investigation. You may package that investigation in either of the
two pathways below.

The pathways differ in **format**, not in the research standard. Both require a
clear question, engagement with prior work, a point-in-time data design, a
feasible baseline, time-aware evaluation, a decision layer, uncertainty,
limitations, citations, and reproducible analysis.

## Pathway 1 — Integrated Jupyter research report

Use one executed Jupyter notebook as both the analysis and the written report.
This is the required-core route and can be completed in JupyterLab or Google
Colab without installing Quarto, LaTeX, or another publishing system.

The notebook must read as a paper from top to bottom. A sequence of code cells
with short comments is not a report. Use substantive Markdown sections so that
a reader can understand the research without reconstructing the argument from
the code.

Required notebook structure:

1. **Title and abstract** — question, data, method, principal result, and main
   limitation.
2. **Introduction** — motivation, intended decision, contribution, and headline
   finding.
3. **Related literature** — what has been studied, what was found, and how the
   project replicates, extends, compares, or stress-tests that work.
4. **Data and information clock** — sources, sample, availability, target
   interval, transformations, and exclusions.
5. **Research design** — hypothesis, baseline, candidate method, data roles,
   search space, primary metric, uncertainty procedure, and lockbox rule.
6. **Results** — baseline first, then incremental model evidence. Every figure
   and table must be introduced and interpreted in prose.
7. **Decision and implementation** — how the estimate becomes a position,
   threshold, price, review rule, or other action, including relevant costs and
   constraints.
8. **Robustness and alternative explanations** — a small set of declared
   sensitivity checks rather than an unrestricted search for a favorable result.
9. **Limitations and conclusion** — what the evidence does and does not
   establish, what could fail in practice, and the direct answer to the research
   question.
10. **References, reproducibility, and AI-use disclosure.**

Submit the executed `.ipynb`. A static HTML export is strongly encouraged for a
portfolio because an employer can read it without running the notebook, but the
export does not replace the notebook for grading.

## Pathway 2 — Analysis plus a separate paper

Use Jupyter notebooks or scripts to acquire data, conduct the analysis, and
generate final figures and tables. Present the argument in a separate paper
written in the tool of your choice, such as:

- Quarto;
- Microsoft Word;
- Google Docs;
- Typst;
- LaTeX; or
- another editor approved by the instructor.

No particular publishing tool earns credit. The final paper should normally be
about **6–10 pages**, excluding references and technical appendices, and should
follow the same intellectual structure required of the integrated notebook.
Export it to a stable format that can be read without specialized software,
normally PDF or self-contained HTML.

The analysis and the paper must remain connected: each reported result should
be generated by recoverable code, and figure/table filenames should make that
connection easy to inspect.

### Optional professional-package bonus

Pathway 2 is eligible for **up to 2 bonus points on the final-project score**,
capped at 100 and counted within the syllabus's overall bonus limit. The bonus
is for a genuinely useful professional package, not for using Quarto or for
decorative formatting. To earn it, submit all of the following:

- a polished standalone paper;
- a polished slide deck derived from the paper;
- a repository landing page containing the abstract, headline result, one key
  figure, links to the paper and slides, data-access notes, and concise
  reproduction instructions; and
- clear separation among exploratory work, final analysis, and generated
  artifacts.

Weak research in an attractive template does not earn the bonus. Pathway 1 can
still receive full project credit.

## Literature standard for both pathways

The literature review is part of the research design, not decoration added
after the result is known. Use prior work to establish the question, choose
baselines and metrics, identify known failure modes, and state what the present
project adds.

As a practical guide, most projects will use **approximately 6–10 credible
sources**, including primary academic research or authoritative technical work
where it exists. Some engineering or emerging topics may have less direct
literature; state that honestly and review the closest work on the problem,
method, data, and evaluation design. A project does not need to claim academic
novelty.

Maintain a literature map while developing the project:

| Source | Question and evidence | Main finding | Relevance to this project |
|---|---|---|---|
| Author (year) | Data, population, and design | Narrow supported finding | Replicate, extend, compare, or challenge |

Verify every citation yourself. Use a recognized citation style consistently,
provide persistent links or DOIs where available, and cite datasets, software,
pretrained models, and other research inputs as well as papers.

## What an employer should be able to see

Regardless of pathway, a reader should be able to identify within a few minutes:

- the financial question and why it matters;
- what information was available at the decision time;
- the baseline and experimental comparison;
- the headline result and its uncertainty;
- the decision or business consequence;
- the principal limitation; and
- how to inspect or reproduce the work.
